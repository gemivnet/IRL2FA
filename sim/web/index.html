Click a device (or Tab to it) and use ← ↑ → ↓ and Enter. Hold a direction to repeat.

  1. On both devices choose Pair with someone, then switch on Hold devices together. Slide them apart past about 9 cm and frames start to fail.
  2. Check the six symbols match on both screens and press the joystick on each.
  3. Name the contact. Now one device's SAY word is the other's HEAR word.

Time

--

Both devices share this UTC clock. Use the skew sliders under each device to drift one clock by up to a minute: words change every 15 s, and each device also accepts the previous word (“or …”), so small skews still verify. While you hold a button the simulation runs in real time. E-paper refreshes take their real time too: a full refresh flashes for 3 s, a partial one 0.42 s, and presses during a refresh are queued as on the device.

    About IRL2FA

    IRL2FA is an open-source, air-gapped hardware token the size of a stick of gum: a 2.13″ e-paper screen, a 5-way joystick, an infrared link and a CR2450 coin cell. It has no USB data, no radio and no network.

    Person-to-person verification. Two people pair their devices once, in person, over IR (an X25519 key agreement checked by comparing six symbols on both screens). From then on each device shows a pair of words that changes every 15 seconds: the word you say and the word you should hear. When a call or message claims to be from them, ask for the word. A voice clone or a hijacked account can't know it.

    Standard TOTP. It is also an ordinary authenticator for the 6- or 8-digit codes websites use (RFC 6238), entered by hand from the base32 secret and kept encrypted on the device.

    Joystick only. Up, down, left, right and press are the whole interface, PIN included.

    This simulator runs the same C firmware as the device (AGPL-3.0), compiled to WebAssembly, on a virtual board. Nothing you do here leaves your browser.
